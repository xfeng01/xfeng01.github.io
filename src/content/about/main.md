I am a first-year Ph.D. student in Computer Science at Georgia Tech.

Previously, I was a research assistant at the [Data-Driven Decision Intelligence (D3I) Lab](https://haipeng-chen.github.io/), led by Prof. Haipeng Chen, at William & Mary (2025–2026). I received my M.S. degree in Electrical and Computer Engineering from UCLA (2023–2025), where I was a member of the [Wireless Lab at UCLA](https://wireless.ee.ucla.edu/), supervised by Prof. [Ian P. Roberts](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=RDAx4ycAAAAJ&hl=en&oi=ao). Before that, I obtained my B.Eng. in Communication Engineering from Chongqing University (2019–2023).

My research interests include reinforcement learning, generative models, and large language models. Currently, I focus primarily on diffusion language models (DLMs), with particular interests in:

- discrete DLMs: decoding order and remasking policies
- continuous DLMs: latent representations, architecture design, efficiency, and parallel generation
- multimodal DLMs: how semantics emerge during generation, and modality binding
- training and testing: training objectives, train–test mismatch, and test-time scaling
- theoretical analysis: learnability and global consistency

Some questions I'm currently exploring:

- What makes a generative process learnable?
- How do representations shape learning and generation?

I enjoy exchanging research ideas and sharing what I've learned. Feel free to reach out if you'd like to discuss research or explore potential collaborations.
